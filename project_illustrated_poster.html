Paintstorm Studio — Project — Illustrated Poster

🎨 Paintstorm 🖼️ Project 🎯 Capstone

This is your capstone. Everything from the Advanced course — brushes, adjustments, perspective, masks, and a tuned workspace — comes together into one finished, print-ready A3 poster with a clean value hierarchy and punchy color.

Goal: Plan, light, and finish an A3 illustrated poster with a clean value hierarchy and punchy color, applying everything from the Advanced course.

Plan the poster

A poster succeeds or fails before you lay a stroke. It must read from across a room, communicate one idea, and reward a closer look. Spend real time here — planning is the cheapest place to fix a composition.

Brief & audience

Write a one-sentence brief and pin it to the top of your file: "A moody night-market poster that makes a viewer want to visit." Decide the audience and the read distance — an event poster on a wall is scanned in two seconds, so the focal point and any type must survive being squinted at. Note the single idea; anything that does not serve it is a candidate for cutting.

Thumbnails

Sketch six to ten tiny thumbnails, each no larger than a matchbook. Work only in silhouette and big value masses — no detail. You are hunting for a strong value pattern and a clear focal point, not a finished drawing. Pick the two strongest, then push those a little further before committing.

A3 canvas setup

Set up print-correct from the start so nothing has to be rebuilt later. A3 is 297 × 420 mm. At 300 dpi that is roughly 3508 × 4961 px. Create the canvas in that size, sRGB or your print profile, and keep a margin.

SettingValueWhy
Canvas size3508 × 4961 pxA3 (297 × 420 mm) at 300 dpi — true print resolution.
Resolution300 dpiStandard for crisp offset and inkjet printing.
OrientationPortraitClassic poster proportion; swap if the brief calls for it.
Safe margin~10 mm insetKeep focal content off the trim edge.
Bleed (if printing full-bleed)+3 mm all sidesExtend background art past trim so no white edge shows.
Color spacesRGB / print profileConsistent color from screen to press.

⚠️ Pitfall: Starting on a screen-sized 1920 px canvas "to save memory" and scaling up later gives you soft, mushy edges at print size. Start at full A3 resolution.

Value plan & focal hierarchy

Assign a deliberate value structure before painting: pick where your lightest light and darkest dark will meet — that highest-contrast point is your focal magnet, and there should be only one. Group everything else into two or three simple value families so the eye reads big shapes first. A common, reliable plan is a dark frame around a lit focal zone.

If your poster reads clearly as a squinted, three-value grayscale thumbnail, color will only make it better. If it does not, no amount of rendering or color will save it.

Step-by-step build

Work top-down: big shapes and value first, refinement last. Save incrementally (poster_v001.psd, poster_v002.psd…) at every phase.

1 · Block-in

On a mid-value background, lay in the largest shapes with a big opaque brush at 100% flow. Work in grayscale or a very limited palette so you judge value honestly. Match the value pattern from your chosen thumbnail — do not add detail yet. Keep foreground, midground, and background on separate layers so you can adjust depth later.

2 · Rendering with the advanced brush engine

Switch to your Render workspace and bring in the tuned brushes from Advanced Brush Engine. Use dual-tip and textured brushes for surface variety, tilt/rotation curves for natural mark direction, and the mixer to soften transitions in shadow while keeping edges crisp at the focal point. Control your edges deliberately — hard edges pull the eye, soft edges recede.

✅ Tip: Reserve your hardest, highest-contrast edges for the focal area only. Everywhere else, let edges lose and soften so nothing competes.

3 · Mood via gradient maps & adjustments

With values solid, unify color using the techniques from Gradient Maps & Adjustments. Drop a gradient map over the whole piece (cool darks, warm lights is a dependable night-market recipe), then use Curves and HSL as stacked, reversible adjustment layers to push contrast and saturation toward the focal point. Keep these non-destructive so you can dial the mood without repainting.

4 · Light FX & edge glow

Add the atmosphere pass using Advanced Masks, FX & Layers. On a layer set to Add/Screen, paint warm light bloom around lamps and signs, add edge glow where a rim light catches a silhouette, and use compound masks so glow spills only where it should. A whisper of this goes a long way — the focal point should be the brightest, warmest spot in the frame.

⚠️ Pitfall: Global glow or a heavy overlay flattens contrast and kills the value hierarchy you worked to build. Mask FX tightly and check the grayscale after.

5 · Perspective-guided structure

Wherever built structure appears — buildings, stalls, signage, floor tiles — stabilize it with Perspective Guides & Rulers. Set one- or two-point guides matching your horizon, use ellipse guides for lanterns and wheels, and let the guides keep converging lines honest. Correct structure now, while shapes are still simple, is far easier than fixing a wonky building after rendering.

6 · Final unify & punch

Step back into your Review workspace, flip the canvas horizontally to catch drawing errors, and squint to confirm the value read still holds. Add a top adjustment for final contrast and a subtle vignette to seat the focal point. Confirm the poster still works at thumbnail size — that is how your audience will first see it.

Self-check: desaturate the whole file to grayscale. Can you still find the focal point in under a second? If not, widen the value gap there before you add any more color.

Export & review

A3 print settings

Deliver a print-ready file, not just a web JPEG. Confirm the document is still 3508 × 4961 px at 300 dpi, then export according to what the printer asks for.

SettingValueNotes
Dimensions3508 × 4961 pxTrue A3 at 300 dpi; verify before flatten.
FormatTIFF or high-quality PDFBest for print; keep a layered PSD master too.
Color profileEmbed sRGB (or printer's CMYK)Ask the print shop; convert to CMYK only if they require it.
Bleed+3 mm all sidesOnly if the design runs full-bleed.
Web preview~1500 px longest edge, sRGB JPEGSeparate smaller export for sharing online.

Color & flatten checklist

  • Merge a flattened copy — never flatten your only master. Keep the layered PSD.
  • Confirm all adjustment layers are on and reading correctly on the merged copy.
  • Check the darkest darks are not pure black clipping and brightest lights are not blown out.
  • Soft-proof if your printer provides a profile; watch saturated warms that may shift in CMYK.
  • Zoom to 100% and scan for stray brush marks, hard mask edges, or leftover guide layers.

Self-review rubric

Grade your own poster honestly against these criteria before you call it done.

CriterionWhat good looks like
Value hierarchyReads clearly in grayscale; one obvious focal point; big shapes group into 2–3 value families.
Focal clarityHighest contrast and sharpest edges land only on the focal area; the eye goes there first.
Color & moodUnified palette with intentional temperature; saturation peaks at the focal point, not everywhere.
Edge controlDeliberate mix of hard and soft edges; background edges lose, focal edges hold.
StructurePerspective is consistent; built forms sit believably in space with no drifting verticals.
Light FXGlow and atmosphere support the focal point without flattening overall contrast.
Read at distanceStill communicates its one idea as a small thumbnail and from across a room.
Print readinessCorrect A3 size, 300 dpi, bleed/margin handled, layered master saved.

✅ Tip: Sit on the finished file overnight, then open the thumbnail cold in the morning. First-glance reactions expose weak focal reads that tired eyes miss.

Skills applied, and where you learned them: block-in and rendering draw on the Advanced Brush Engine; mood and unity come from Gradient Maps & Adjustments; structure is stabilized with Perspective Guides & Rulers; atmosphere and edge glow use Advanced Masks, FX & Layers; and the whole build stays fast and comfortable thanks to Custom Workspaces & Hotkeys.
